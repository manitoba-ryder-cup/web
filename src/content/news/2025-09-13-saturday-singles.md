Blue came to the singles needing four and a half points from twelve and had eight by the end of the
afternoon. The cup was gone early and never came back. Nigel Milnes, who lifted it, lost three of
the four matches he played.

The last of them was to Travis Bale, which will not have improved the taste. Level after ten, Bale
won the eleventh, twelfth and thirteenth to go three up and came home two up. Milnes ends the week
beaten 5 & 3, then 3 & 2, then by two, with a Saturday-morning win alongside Jon Ray the only thing
standing between him and the whole set.

Evan Martin had the best week in red and it still came to nothing. A year on from captaining the
winning side by a single point, he took three of his four and lost the other by one hole. Here he
was four up on Iain McDonald by the eighth, pegged back to one by the twelfth, then took three of
the last four for a 4 & 2.

Two matches reached the eighteenth. Stu Shiells led Harman Kaler from the fourth hole to the
sixteenth, squared on the seventeenth and was beaten on the last. Garry Hargreaves led Justin Rabe
by two after eight, was level again by the fifteenth and lost the sixteenth and the eighteenth to go
down by one — four matches and four defeats, a week Matt Phin matched exactly. Rabe went the other
way and won all four of his.

Mike Kalic, who got through Friday's fourball without winning a hole on his own, beat Ryan Zaporzan
4 & 2 and was not behind at any point in it.

The rest of Blue's afternoon was routine. Jon Ray beat Harbs Benning 2 & 1 and Blair Horn did the
same to Matt Phin; Keith Van Walleghem took Adam Sefton 3 & 2; Matt Pilloud beat Connor Macaulay 4 &
3 and Ian Fordyce beat Erik St-Hilaire by the same; Jason Gunnlaugson closed out Dusty McMillan 5 &
4, the widest margin of the day. Rohan Lall took Red's fourth point, 2 & 1 over Brad Zander.

**Blue 19, Red 11.** Four and a half points needed and eight taken, turning a cup that had been one
point apart after the opening day into a margin that suggests it was never close.
