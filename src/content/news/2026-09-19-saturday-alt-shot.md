Four of the six alternate-shot matches were decided by the fifteenth hole, and the two that went the
distance both went to the eighteenth. It was that kind of morning: nothing in the middle.

Ian Fordyce and Rohan Lall were two down to Trevor Horn and Ryan Zaporzan after twelve, won the
thirteenth and fourteenth to square it, went ahead at the fifteenth, were pegged back at the
sixteenth, and won the eighteenth to take it by a hole. Lall has three wins from three. Horn and
Zaporzan led that match from the ninth to the thirteenth and have nothing to show for it.

Travis Bale and Evan Martin were six up on Mike Kalic and Jon Ray after nine and closed it on the
thirteenth, 6 & 5. Kalic came into the morning having won both his matches in twenty-seven holes;
this one took thirteen and he lost it. The Blue captain has now lost two of his three with a
partner.

Blue answered with the same margin an hour later — 7 & 6, the widest of the week. Garry Hargreaves
and Dustin Mansfield won the first hole on Keith Van Walleghem and Brad Zander, never gave it back,
were seven up after twelve, and that was the match. Zander has now been on the wrong end of a 7 & 6
twice in two days.

Blair Horn and Stu Shiells led Nigel Milnes and Matt Phin from the first hole and were never level
again, closing it 5 & 3. Shiells has three wins from three and Milnes three defeats from three.

Aaron Challis and Connor Macaulay were five up on Dan McInnes and Matt Pilloud by the ninth and gave
nothing back worth counting, 5 & 3. Challis has won all three of his.

Harbs Benning and Iain McDonald halved six of the first eight with Dusty McMillan and Justin Rabe,
then won the ninth, tenth, thirteenth and fifteenth to take it 4 & 3. The Red captain's first win of
the week, at the third attempt.

**Blue 9½, Red 8½.** Twelve singles this afternoon settle it. Blue need six of them, Red need seven.
